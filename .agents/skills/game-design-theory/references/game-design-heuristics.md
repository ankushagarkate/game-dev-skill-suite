# Game Design Heuristics & Analysis Reference

## Crawford's Four Common Game Design Flaws
1. **Pretentiousness**: Disguising weak core gameplay behind elaborate narrative or thematic dressing.
2. **Design by Committee**: Compromising a singular artistic vision to satisfy conflicting requirements.
3. **Imitation without Understanding**: Copying mechanics from successful games without understanding the systemic dynamics that made them fun.
4. **Complexity for its own Sake**: Adding rules and parameters that inflate cognitive load without creating meaningful strategic depth.

## The MDA Diagnostic Cycle
When playtesters report feeling bored, anxious, or confused:
```
Player symptom (Aesthetic) 
       ↓ 
Trace to emergent behavior (Dynamic) 
       ↓ 
Isolate and adjust base rules/parameters (Mechanic)
```

- **Symptom: "The game feels like a grind"**
  - Dynamic: Optimal strategy is repetitive, low-risk, low-reward farming.
  - Mechanic Fix: Introduce diminishing returns, escalate risk-reward multipliers, or gate progression behind skill tests rather than item counts.
- **Symptom: "I don't know what I did wrong"**
  - Dynamic: Player dies without seeing the cause.
  - Mechanic Fix: Add telegraph frames, pre-attack audio cues, or reduce one-hit kill mechanics.
- **Symptom: "Choice feels meaningless"**
  - Dynamic: All options lead to the identical dominant strategy.
  - Mechanic Fix: Differentiate opportunity costs, add rock-paper-scissors counters, or force resource trade-offs.

## Bartle Archetype Interaction Dynamics
- **Achievers vs. Killers**: Killers prey on Achievers; Achievers demand protection (safe zones, opt-in PvP) or quit.
- **Explorers vs. Achievers**: Explorers uncover content that Achievers then optimize and farm.
- **Socializers**: Stabilize the community; their retention depends on low-friction group coordination tools.
