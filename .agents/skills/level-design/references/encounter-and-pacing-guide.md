# Encounter Pacing & Level Design Reference

## Kevin Lynch Spatial Elements Applied to 3D Levels
1. **Paths**: Primary transit channels (hallways, trails, tracks) with high legibility and clear flow vectors.
2. **Edges**: Linear boundaries organizing transitions (cliffs, ravines, barricades, waterfronts) that prevent disorientation.
3. **Districts**: Distinct spatial zones sharing visual themes, lighting palettes, and audio ambience.
4. **Nodes**: Focal junction points where decisions are made (crossroads, plazas, elevator landings).
5. **Landmarks**: Prominent visual beacons visible from multiple perspectives to maintain orientation without UI markers.

## Encounter Template Checklist
When staging a combat or puzzle encounter:
- [ ] **Player Entrance Sightline**: Can the player view the arena, threats, and cover before committing?
- [ ] **Cover Hierarchy**: High cover (break line of sight), low cover (crouch and shoot), destructible cover.
- [ ] **Flank Vectors**: At least two routes to approach high-threat positions (e.g., sniper nests).
- [ ] **Height Advantage**: Elevated perches with clear climbing signifiers (ledges, ladders).
- [ ] **Resource Placement**: Ammo and health positioned in contested zones to force movement out of turtling.
- [ ] **Exit Gate / Breather**: Clear visual transition to safe zone following encounter resolution.

## Greybox Color Code Standard
- `Grey (50%)`: Neutral structural geometry (floors, walls, ceilings).
- `Orange`: Player spawn point and primary critical path indicator.
- `Blue`: Interactive props (doors, switches, levers, climbable ladders).
- `Green`: Health, ammo, and collectible reward positions.
- `Red`: Hazards, kill planes, and enemy spawn locations.
- `Yellow`: Choke points, encounter threshold triggers, and objective markers.
